## Fact 1

The conversion from Kilometres to Miles is extremely close to the number for the golden ratio phi (Φ), around 1.618 where the number for the conversion is 1.609. Using this knowledge, and saying I know I am to walk 34 kilometres tomorrow, I know very quickly that a good approximation for the distance in miles is 21, because I know my golden ration numbers. (Golden ratio numbers are built by starting with 1 and 2 and then summing the last 2 terms for the next one, so 1+2 = 3 2+3 = 5 5+3 = 8). The bigger the terms the closer you get to phi when putting the last term on top of the previous. However, you are already very close even at 5/3 as this is 1.666. <br>

An even simpler conversion is adding a half and a tenth to the miles. For example If I am walking 10 miles, a half and a tenth would be 5 and 1 and therefore I will walk 16 kilometres. This is a simpler conversion as all you are really doing is multiplying by 1.6. 

## Fact 2

The second Fact I find interesting is the amount of distance you would need to walk to change time zones. After calculations, I found while at the equator, you would need to walk around 28 km to move one 1 min around, meaning the true time would be 1 min different from your origin. This is reduced as you move nearer to the poles, as the radius of the slice of earth there reduces. At the tropics, I found it to be closer to 25 km per min, and in London, where I live, you would only need to walk 17 km per 1. <br>

Therefore, you could escape the night by travelling 17 km per minute, or 283 m/s (a bit under speed of sound) laterally West forever, never seeing the night, assuming the earths tilt and bulbous shape don't throw off my very rudimentary calculations.
