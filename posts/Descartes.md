## Descartes Arrival

Descartes uses this line within his conclusion of his book 'Disourse on the Method'. He starts be attempting to answer the question of how we find truths within the sciences, developing into a question of how we know something to be the true. To answer this question, Descartes asks what he knows to be true, starting to strip back everything that could be false. He knows his senses could decieve him, like a hallucination, and therefore can't guarantee his sense data to be true. He also discovers that he can't fully trust that his thoughts are truly his and haven't been planted into his head. Descartes continues on this road until he imagines a demon, but unlike Laplace's demon, this demon aimed to decept and decieve. He thought that if everything he expereinced was a trick by this demon, then he could not trust any of this. Slowly, slowly, Descartes realises that to even be able to be tricked he must at least 'be'. Therefore all he knows for certian is that he is. Therefore, he happens upon the phrase 'I think therefore I am ' or as he meant it more 'I am thinking there for I am being'

## Descartes Meaning and its Misconceptions

A common misconception is that Descartes meant that anyone who can think can guarantee their existance. This is very much not true, Descartes purely meant that I you are able to think/ acknowledge your existance, then you can guarantee that you, and only you, exist in that exact moment. Here another point is made, the acknowledgement of existance is a very present thing, you only know of your definite existance in the moment that you think. This point links back to the specificity of 'I am thinking', putting more emphasis on the current moment. 

## Descartes Continuation into a proof for a God

Descartes now uses this line of thinking to inspect his thoughts and categorize his ideas. Descartes believes in a rule of causality, where an cause must have at least as much reality or perfection as its effect. Using this rule, and the fact that Descartes believes himself to be an imperfect, finite and incomplete being, Descartes concludes that for the idea of perfection to be existant in his brain, there must be a complete and perfect being that caused it and planted it in his brain, as due to the rule of causality, he could not have created this idea.
