## The Original Philosopher

Using the goal posts of what qualified a Philosopher as a Philosopher in ancient history, many, if not all, people on earth would be considered Philosophers. Is this true? Could everyone be considered a Philosopher? <br>

Firstly, I think it is prevalent to think back to the first Philosopher and ask why he was classified as a Philosopher. Thales, as wrote about in my article on the Presocratic, is widely considered the first Philosopher. People classify him as such due to his questioning of the world around him. However, instead of using guess work to uncover the answers to these questions, he looked at and considered evidence in aid to reach a conclusion. 

## Breaking down Definitions

Therefore, could you say that doing Philosophy is using and considering evidence as you build to a conclusion to a question? You may firstly have a qualm with this answer as to what Philosophy is due to its vagueness. What about Physicians, or Historians, or Chemists? They all use evidence in a push for conclusions? This is true, and is also why early thinkers who used to study nature (what we would call physicist) were called natural philosophers. Nearly every modern day core academic pursuit stems from a study through a Philosophical lens.<br>

Therefore, when a detective investigates a crime, taking evidence and facts into consideration toward an end conclusion, are they being Philosophical? I would be inclined to say no, I believe Philosophers to study more general, permanent facts rather than every day questions. <br>

Another popular definition, although I believe it to be retrofitted, is that Philosophy is a 'love and pursuit of wisdom'. While this is probably true nowadays, it is not the act but the reason behind the study of philosophy. Many if not all people on earth would like to know one more fact than another person or to be smarter than the room. This however does not make them Philosophers, due to the attraction to wisdom. 

## Final Conclusions

I think the final definition I land on would be - 'a love for asking questions about general and longer standing topics, using evidence and logic to reach a reasoned answer'. I think this balances a line of being accessible but not common practice.
